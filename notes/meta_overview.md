# Cobblemon Delta Ranked Meta Overview

## Scope / Source Note

These notes are based on our ranked ladder testing, Discord/meta discussion, usage screenshots, move-learnset screenshots, patch-note reactions, and team-building work. This is not a complete official database. Treat it as a migration file for what we currently know, what we have tested, and what conclusions we have been using.

For **live usage data** see `notes/usage_stats.md` (Season 6 mid, 1500+ bracket snapshot 2026-05-12 from `ranked.cobblemondelta.com`). That file is authoritative for "what's actually being played" and overrides any contradicting viability claim in this doc.

## Current Competitive Context

Cobblemon Delta Ranked is a custom Cobblemon metagame with vanilla Pokémon, Delta Pokémon, fusion Pokémon, ATOM forms, custom abilities, custom signature moves, custom Z-moves/Z-crystals, Battle Point investment systems, and a GTS economy where rarity, ranked viability, IVs, hidden abilities, and event accessibility heavily affect price.

The ladder is highly matchup-sensitive at the top. Building only for general strength works up to Top 100, but Top 20 and higher requires: clear plan versus aggressive leads, anti-hazard tools, status control, priority management, ability to handle custom wallbreakers, defensive glue that does more than just take hits, and win conditions that are not all stopped by the same defensive core.

## Ranked Progress / Player Context

The main ranked team reached Top 100 after climbing to around 1409 rating and later Top 20 in the new season. A lot of these takes come from repeated ranked testing into real high-ladder teams, including the former #3/#1 player.

## Major Meta Themes

### Custom threats define the meta more than vanilla logic

Many Pokémon have custom moves, custom abilities, custom forms, or altered roles. Vanilla Smogon instincts help, but they are not enough. Examples include Tangled Time using Upgrade as a Booster Energy replacement, Terapagos-ATOM having multi-form/Z-move mechanics, Tinkaton-Gamma setting Delta Stream through Parasol Prayer, Draculedge having Conviction and Twin Cross, Secret Armor using Protosynthesis and Ironclad Guard, Delta Marshadow commonly running Regenerator, Clefgar using Poison Heal, and Iron Sentinel becoming much stronger in rain.

### Defensive glue is extremely valuable

Pure offense gets punished if it lacks ways to absorb Mega Lopunny Scrappy pressure, Gholdengo-Delta Z-move pressure, hazards, burns/status, rain offense, stall cores, and bulky Water/Flying/Steel cores. Good glue examples from our builds include Tinkaton-Gamma, Gliscor, Corviknight, Secret Armor, Clefgar, Garganacl, Ferrothorn on rain, and Alomomola on bulkier structures.

### Hazard game is unstable because of Gholdengo-style denial / bounce

Sticky Web was strong but became less reliable once high-ladder opponents abused U-turn into Gholdengo-Delta, hazard denial/bounce interactions, sleep nerfs, and aggressive anti-lead play. Shrookle looked strong into average teams but unreliable into better players.

### Top-ladder meta rewards flexible teams more than cheese

A one-matchup cheese answer can win games, but a consistent team needs a safe lead plan, a backup plan if hazards fail, multiple win conditions, at least one strong priority or speed-control tool, and at least one bulky pivot/glue.

### Physical overload is a real teambuilding trap

Rain teams especially can accidentally stack too many physical attackers: Barraskewda, Urshifu-Rapid-Strike, Ogerpon-Wellspring, and Mega Swampert. Better players can pivot around them with Skarmory, Corviknight, Alomomola, Rocky Helmet users, burns, and resist cycling. Rain needs either a special rain breaker, a better defensive glue slot, or both. Iron Sentinel is attractive because it gives rain a special breaker angle.

## High-Level Viability Takeaways

### Strong / Meta-Relevant

Ogerpon-Wellspring, Tinkaton-Gamma, Tangled Time, Draculedge, Secret Armor, Gliscor, Corviknight, Delta Marshadow, Archapult, Clefgar, Terapagos-ATOM, Iron Sentinel in rain, Urshifu-Rapid-Strike, Mega Scizor, Mega Lopunny, Gholdengo-Delta, Skarmory, Tapukehe, Ferrothorn in rain, and Alomomola in rain/balance.

### Strong but Context-Dependent

Heatran/Heatran-Delta, Eruptois, Shrookle, Garganacl, Spectreon, Mewrai, Iron Coil, Golurk-Delta, Ursasola, Magigrigus, Valiant Time/Iron Valiant style HO pieces, and Kyurem hyper offense pieces.

## Economy-Driven / Investment Notes

Tangled Time was around 1.8m–2m for \~81–82% IV versions. Landorus-Therian hundo at 1.25m looked like a strong investment but was later sold. Secret Armor was rare on GTS, historical Discord sales around 500k, bought for 1.5m summon and BP-capped to 94%. Iron Sentinel looked overpriced at 6.5m but around 1m becomes much more reasonable for rain. Draculedge initially had extreme GTS value around 9m due to ranked reward exclusivity but expected to depreciate once more players reached 300 ranked battles. Regular Heatran can be cheaper to use if Flash Fire avoids BP investment compared with Heatran-Delta requiring BP fixes.

## Important Ranked Lessons Learned

1. Do not overcommit to one ladder matchup, even if the #1 player destroys you. Build for the field unless the matchup is everywhere.
2. If a Pokémon only works when one condition is active, price it lower unless your team is fully built around that condition.
3. Keep immunities in mind. Removing the only Dragon immunity can make a team worse even if the new mon is stronger on paper.
4. Priority and speed control matter more in practice than raw BST.
5. Hazard plans need backup lines because top players punish predictable Webs/Rocks.
6. A team with two clear win conditions plus four support/glue slots often performs better than six strong mons.
7. If a custom ability/item interaction is unclear, ask mods. Upgrade functioned as Booster Energy replacement for Tangled Time because Booster sprite was missing.
8. Z-crystal mechanics are important enough to change a team report if the Z-move changes the mon’s form or role.
